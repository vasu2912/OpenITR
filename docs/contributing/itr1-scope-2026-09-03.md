## Source precedence decisions

The notified AY 2026-27 form controls the current income envelope. The published validation rules corroborate its limits. Historical instructions supply unchanged exclusions where the form's short heading is not exhaustive.

The current [individual return guidance](https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/individual/return-applicable-1) still describes one house property and a total-income limit that excludes section 112A gains. Those statements do not control this revision:

- Notification 45/2026, clause 2(a)(i)(B), replaces "one house property" with "two house properties" in Rule 12(1)(a)(ii).
- The notified form's heading on Gazette page 16 permits two house properties and section 112A long-term capital gains up to ₹1,25,000.
- The note under Part C, C2, on Gazette page 18 expressly includes those gains in the ₹50,00,000 total-income ceiling.
- The AY 2026-27 validation rules corroborate the ₹5,000 agricultural-income boundary in rule 29 and the ₹1,25,000 section 112A boundary in rule 217.

The historical instructions' one-property limit and blanket capital-gains exclusion are superseded. Neither becomes an executable rule in this revision. The current [ITR-1 FAQ](https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/all-topics/e-filing-services/ITR1-FAQ?mobile-app=1) corroborates the two-property limit and section 112A exception, but is explanatory evidence rather than the source of those rules.

## Scope coverage

The boundaries below describe OpenITR's analysis-scope decision, not an official determination. All required facts remain unresolved until supplied. A missing fact is not an exclusion and never becomes a negative answer by default.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Taxpayer and residence | Individual, resident other than not ordinarily resident | Notified form, Gazette page 16 heading |
| Total income | At most ₹50,00,000, including permitted section 112A gains | Notified form, Gazette pages 16 and 18, Part C C2 note |
| House properties | Zero, one, or two | Notification clause 2(a)(i)(B); notified form heading |
| Section 112A gains | Long-term gains at most ₹1,25,000 | Notified form heading; validation rule 217 |

Salary from more than one employer, salary pension, ordinary dividends, family pension, the permitted property income, small agricultural income, and the permitted section 112A gains are not blanket exclusions. Either tax regime can be in scope. Clubbed spouse or minor-child income is not itself an exclusion. It must meet the same income categories and limits, including the separate other-person TDS condition. Historical instructions section 2, NOTE, supplies this distinction.

A current-year house-property loss is not automatically a carried-forward loss. The scope question must distinguish those facts rather than reject all property losses.

## Evidence and product boundaries

Each scope decision retains its supplied or unresolved fact, rule citation, deterministic explanation, and pinned rule-pack identity. The identity includes both source-manifest and compiled-pack hashes. Conflicted or blocked facts remain distinct from missing facts and known exclusions.

A total-income answer is not inferred from a salary observation, an AIS entry, or a document total. The existing prefilled adapter supplies a reviewed subset of salary and TDS observations, not the complete scope facts. The existing missing-fact questionnaire supplies only the facts its rule pack permits.

The checklist describes expected official evidence and unresolved questions. It does not promise that an adapter can extract every field from an expected document. Private bank, lender, payroll, and broker templates remain unsupported. Property, agricultural-income, and capital-gains scope support does not add a parser or a tax computation for those categories.

Educational limitations remain separate from the deterministic rule result. No supported result grants permission to file, determines official eligibility, establishes filing readiness, or predicts portal acceptance.
